### Part 1: Where Did We Get It Wrong? (Trait Score vs. Decision Weight)

In economics and psychology (Keeney & Raiffa’s *Multi-Attribute Utility Theory*), there is a massive mathematical difference between two things:

1. **The Trait Level / Magnitude ($x_i \in [1, 10]$):**  
   *"How much of this trait does Ramesh have in his personality?"*  
   *(e.g., Ramesh has a **9/10 Human Recourse Need** and a **7/10 Tax / Paper Trail Anxiety**).* This is his permanent DNA.
2. **The Decision Relevance Weight ($w_i \in [0, 1]$):**  
   *"How much does THIS specific product decision actually involve that trait?"*

#### The Mistake We Made Previously:
We assumed that if Ramesh has a **7/10 in Tax Paper Trail Anxiety**, that anxiety should blast at **100% volume ($w = 1.0$) on EVERY question he is ever asked!**

Look at how absurd that is in the real world:
* When Ramesh evaluates a **Doctor’s Video Consultation**:
  * *Human Recourse / Physical Stethoscope:* Relevance Weight = **$0.95$** (Massive).
  * *Tax / Paper Trail Anxiety:* Relevance Weight = **$0.00$** (Nobody worries about an income tax audit when their child has a fever!).
* When Ramesh evaluates **Buying an EV Scooter**:
  * *Battery Reliability & Downtime:* Relevance Weight = **$0.90$** (Huge).
  * *Diagnostic Stethoscope:* Relevance Weight = **$0.00$** (Irrelevant).

**Where we got it wrong:** We treated a high personality score as an active veto on *everything*, forgetting that a trait only matters if the product actually touches it!
